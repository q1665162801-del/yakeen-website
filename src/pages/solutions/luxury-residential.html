What we see all the time

3 mistakes villas make on lighting.

We walk into a lot of finished homes and see the same problems. None of them are unfixable, but they're much less expensive to get right the first time.

Mistake #1

Too many downlights, all the same brightness

The ceiling looks like an airport runway and the room feels flat. You need layers — ambient, accent, and task — with different brightness levels. A 2:1 ratio between accent and ambient makes a room feel intentional, not institutional.

Mistake #2

4000K everywhere because "cool white is modern"

Nobody looks good under 4000K at home. 2700K or 3000K max for living areas. Cool white belongs in hospitals and office cubicles, not your living room. Tunable white is an option if you want flexibility — but set the warm end as default.

Mistake #3

Buying fixtures last, after the ceiling is done

Lighting gets specified at the very end, when the ceiling holes are already cut and the electrician is asking for a model number. That's backwards. Good lighting starts with the lighting design — the ceiling layout should follow, not lead.

Building or renovating? Get the lighting right before the drywall goes up.
