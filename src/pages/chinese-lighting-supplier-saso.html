Why SASO Matters

Why SASO certification matters for Chinese suppliers.

Saudi Arabia is the largest construction market in the GCC, and SASO 2902 certification is mandatory for all LED lighting products imported into the Kingdom. Yet many Chinese lighting suppliers either don't have SASO certification, or they have certificates that don't actually cover the products they're selling. This is a problem — and it's one that costs projects time and money.

Rejected Shipments

The Cost of Getting It Wrong

A shipment without proper SASO documentation gets held at customs. Storage fees accrue daily. Eventually the goods are either returned to sender at the shipper's cost, or destroyed. For a project already on a tight schedule, a rejected shipment isn't just an inconvenience — it can push back the entire project timeline.

Hidden Certification Costs

What Suppliers Don't Tell You

Some Chinese suppliers quote a low price, then add certification fees later. Or they provide a single SASO certificate for one model and claim it covers their entire product line. By the time you discover the gap, the project is already underway and changing suppliers would cause delays.

SABER Platform Complexity

Electronic Conformity Assessment

The Saudi Standards, Metrology and Quality Organization (الهيئة السعودية للمواصفات والمقاييس والجودة — SASO) moved to the SABER electronic platform for all product conformity assessment. This means every product needs a Product Conformity Certificate (PCoC) and every shipment needs a Shipment Conformity Certificate (SCoC) — all issued electronically through the SABER system.

Energy Efficiency Requirements

SASO 2902

SASO 2902 is the Saudi energy efficiency standard for LED lighting products. It sets minimum efficacy requirements, classifies products by efficiency grade, and requires registration in the SASO energy efficiency database. Products that don't meet the efficacy requirements can't be legally imported into the Kingdom.

Certification Process

How SASO certification actually works.

SASO certification isn't a one-time test and you're done. It's an ongoing process involving product registration, annual surveillance, and shipment-specific certificates. Understanding the process helps you avoid common pitfalls when sourcing from Chinese suppliers.

01

Product Testing

The first step is testing the product to verify it meets SASO standards. Testing is conducted by a SASO-accredited laboratory following IEC 60598 (safety) and SASO 2902 (energy efficiency) standards. The test report becomes the basis for the certification application.

02

PCoC Issuance

Based on the test report, a SASO-authorized certification body issues a Product Conformity Certificate (PCoC). The PCoC is registered on the SABER platform and is typically valid for one year. Each product model needs its own PCoC — one certificate doesn't cover an entire product line.

03

SABER Registration

The product and PCoC are registered on the SABER electronic platform. Each importer or exporter must have a SABER account to issue shipment certificates. The product registration includes all technical details: model number, wattage, efficacy, CRI, dimensions, and test report references.

04

SCoC for Each Shipment

For every shipment to Saudi Arabia, a Shipment Conformity Certificate (SCoC) must be issued through SABER. The SCoC confirms that the products in the shipment match the registered PCoC. We issue SCoC certificates for every Saudi-bound order before the shipment arrives at port.

05

Annual Surveillance

SASO conducts annual surveillance to ensure products continue to meet the standards. This may include factory audits and sample testing. PCoC certificates must be renewed annually with updated test reports if required. We handle all renewals proactively — no expired certificates on our watch.

06

Customs Clearance

At the port of entry — Jeddah Islamic Port, King Abdulaziz Port Dammam, or Riyadh Dry Port — Saudi Customs verifies the SCoC against the shipment. If everything matches, the shipment is cleared. If there's a discrepancy — wrong model, expired certificate, missing SCoC — the shipment is held.

Common Misconceptions

SASO certification myths debunked.

There's a lot of misinformation floating around about SASO certification, especially among Chinese suppliers who don't specialize in the Saudi market. Here are the most common misconceptions we encounter — and the reality behind them.

"CE covers SASO"

Myth #1

Reality: CE marking does NOT satisfy Saudi requirements. SASO has its own standards, its own testing requirements, and its own certification process. A CE-certified product may or may not pass SASO testing — there's no automatic equivalence. You need actual SASO certification, issued through the SABER system.

"One certificate covers everything"

Myth #2

Reality: Each product model needs its own PCoC. A supplier who shows you one SASO certificate and claims their entire catalog is covered is either misinformed or dishonest. The certificate must match the exact model number, wattage, and specifications of the product being shipped.

"SASO is just paperwork"

Myth #3

Reality: SASO certification requires actual product testing by accredited laboratories. It's not a registration you fill out online. Products must meet specific safety and energy efficiency standards, and the certification body verifies this through testing and factory audits.

"Custom products can't be SASO certified"

Myth #4

Reality: Custom products can be SASO certified — it just takes time and testing. If you have a custom fixture for a Saudi project, we can take it through the SASO certification process. The timeline depends on complexity, but typically runs 4-8 weeks for testing and PCoC issuance.
