The Brief

It started with one chandelier. It ended with the whole house.

This is the pattern in almost every private villa we quote. The first call is about one room. The real project is the other twenty.

The property

Three storeys plus basement, roughly 900 m². A double-height majlis, a six-metre entrance foyer, a gallery corridor — and a garden that had no lighting design at all.

The ask

One chandelier over the majlis. That was the first call. By the second call it was the whole villa, because the owner had started adding up what four suppliers would mean.

The constraint

A fixed handover date. The decorative piece sat on the critical path and nobody had put it there on purpose — it was simply the last thing anyone thought to schedule.

The catch

Three quotes already on the table. Three slightly different “3000K”, two dimming protocols, and nobody willing to own the ceiling interface where the chandelier meets the plaster.

Scope

What was actually in the package.

One BOQ, one colour temperature, one dimming protocol. The decorative and architectural ranges were specified together so they could be commissioned together.

What Decided The Project

Four calls that saved the handover.

Build the chandelier first

Production on the decorative piece started before the downlights. Sequenced last, the villa would have been finished except for the first room anyone walks into.

One CCT, one bin

Everything at 3000K, CRI 95+, MacAdam ≤ 3. Same LED bin for the chandelier and the downlights, so the majlis doesn't show two different whites after dark.

Ship once

The downlights were ready at week six. We held them. One consolidated file, one arrival, one site team. Slightly more storage, far less coordination on site.

Say what we don't do

We supplied the loads and the anchor design; the project structural engineer approved the slab. That was put in writing at week two, so nobody discovered it on installation day.

The Honest Part

What we refused. And what we got wrong.

Refused

Three substitutions

  • A “matching” catalogue chandelier whose 3000K lamp measured 3400K in its own photometric report
  • Chrome plate on the sea-facing terrace fittings — it would have failed within two seasons in Muscat
  • Splitting the shipment to hit a partial date that would have left the villa half-lit for six weeks
Would do differently

Two things

  • Ask for the garden scope in meeting one — it arrived in meeting four and added 22 fittings late
  • Get the ceiling void depth before concept — one interface detail was redrawn twice because the void was 40 mm shallower than assumed
