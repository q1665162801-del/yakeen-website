How to choose

Not sure which downlight? Start here.

Six variants is a lot. Here's how we narrow it down in a real specification — by answering three questions.

01

How important is the ceiling aesthetic?

Trimless if you want the fixture to disappear — specify it early, before the ceiling goes up. Fixed trim if the ceiling is already in and you need a clean, reliable install.

02

What beam angle do you need?

Narrow (15°) for accent lighting on art or features. Medium (36°) for general retail. Wide (60°) for even ambient fill. If you're still figuring it out, start with 36° — it's the workhorse.

03

Will people look up at it?

If the space has high ceilings or people will be directly underneath, go with a deep-recessed anti-glare variant (UGR < 19). Corridors, lobbies, and offices almost always need it.

Still not sure? Send us the ceiling drawing and we'll tell you what to use.

Engineering Deep Dive

How we get UGR < 19 on a trimless downlight.

Anyone can put a diffuser on a downlight and call it "low glare." The trick is doing it without losing half the light output. Here's how our anti-glare optics actually work.

01

Deep-recessed LED position

The LED source sits 38mm below the ceiling plane — deeper than most competitors. More depth = smaller visible angle = less glare. The trade-off is a larger housing, but that's also where the heatsink lives.

02

Black baffle + textured reflector

A matte black baffle absorbs stray light at wide angles. The micro-textured reflector redirects useful light forward while keeping the source invisible from normal viewing positions. Low-grade fixtures skip this and you can see the chip.

03

COB with precise TIR lens

COB LEDs produce a more uniform source than SMD arrays. Paired with a Total Internal Reflection lens, we get a clean beam edge and <UGR 19 at normal ceiling heights.

The result: UGR < 19 at 2.8m ceiling height, 90 lm/W system efficacy, and you can't see the LED source from a 60° viewing angle. If you want the IES file to verify, request it with your project package below.
