How to choose

Three questions before you pick a family.

Exterior selection is decided by mounting condition first and photometry second. Get the first answer wrong and no optic will save the detail.

01

Where does the fixture sit?

On the facade → floodlight or linear wall washer. In paving → in-ground uplight (needs drainage detail). In soil → spike or bollard. In water → IP68 SELV only. The mounting condition sets the IP class before anything else.

02

How far is the surface it lights?

A 12m facade from a 1m setback needs a wide asymmetric optic at high output. A 3m tree from the ground needs 25–40°. Distance and setback decide the beam angle — not the fixture datasheet in isolation.

03

What is the corrosion exposure?

Inland Riyadh is one class; a Jeddah or Dubai waterfront plot is another. Within 1km of the coast, specify 316 stainless fasteners and C5-M/CX finish as a minimum. Salt fog eats a standard powder coat in two seasons.

Engineering Deep Dive

Why an IP66 sticker is not a Gulf specification.

Most exterior failures are not water ingress on day one. They are thermal de-rating in year one, gasket compression in year two, and corrosion at the fastener in year three. Here is what we actually control.

01

Driver de-rating at Ta 55°C

A driver rated 100% at 25°C will typically deliver 60–70% at 55°C — and its electrolytic capacitors lose life at roughly every 10°C. We publish the de-rated output, place the driver in a thermally separated compartment, and specify 105°C-rated capacitors. Fixtures that quote only 25°C data are being honest about a condition that does not exist here.

02

IP66 is a jet test, IP67 an immersion test

IP66 proves resistance to powerful water jets; IP67 proves 30 minutes at 1m depth; IP68 is continuous submersion by agreement. A sand-laden Gulf project needs the gasket compression set, a breather membrane to stop pressure pumping, and a cable gland rated to the same class — the gland is where most "IP66" fixtures actually leak.

03

Corrosion is a finish system, not a paint colour

Chromate conversion coating, then 80µm minimum powder coat, then 316 stainless fasteners and a drainage path that never traps water against the casting. On waterfront plots we go further with a C5-M/CX system. Any fixture that hides a mild-steel screw inside an aluminium body will show rust within two seasons.

The result: published lumen output at 55°C ambient, a sealed optical chamber independent of the driver chamber, and a finish schedule that states the corrosion class in writing. If you want the IP and salt-fog test reports, they are in the spec package below.
