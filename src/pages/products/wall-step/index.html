How to choose

Three answers that pick the family.

Wall and step lighting is mostly decided by the zone and the wall finish, not by wattage.

01

Is it a wet zone?

Bathroom zones 1 and 2 need IP44 minimum; a shower ceiling or steam room needs IP65. Steps and external walls need IP65 regardless of how sheltered they look — cleaning water finds its way in.

02

Do you want the wall lit, or an object on it?

Lit wall → asymmetric wall washer at 100–300mm offset. Object on the wall → narrow accent or up/down so the fixture does not flatten the thing it is meant to reveal.

03

What is the wall finish?

Polished marble shows every scallop; micro-cement and textured plaster hide a lot. On smooth stone, the asymmetric offset optic is mandatory — a symmetric downlight will produce visible cones within a metre.

Engineering Deep Dive

Scalloping is an optics problem, not an installation problem.

When a wall wash shows a row of bright arcs, the usual fix on site is to space the fixtures further apart. That reduces the symptom and leaves the wall unevenly lit. The actual cause sits inside the luminaire.

01

Offset lens, not a diffuser

A symmetric downlight throws a cone at the wall; the arc you see is the cone's top edge. An asymmetric optic uses an offset TIR lens to bend the beam sideways so its long axis runs parallel to the wall plane. The distribution, not the spacing, is what removes the scallop.

02

Setback distance sets uniformity

At 100mm from the wall the wash is high and narrow; at 300mm it is softer and reaches lower. We publish the uniformity ratio for each setback so the detail can be drawn correctly at first fix, rather than adjusted on site with a drill.

03

Grazing angle and wall texture

Below about 15° to the wall plane, light starts to reveal every trowel mark and joint. On fine plaster we aim for 20–30° of grazing; on rough stone or brick we can go shallower on purpose, because the texture is the point.

The result: a published uniformity ratio per setback, an optic that keeps the source hidden from normal viewing angles, and a wall detail that works on polished stone as well as textured plaster. Ask for the wall-wash calculation with the IES file.
