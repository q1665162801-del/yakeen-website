The Qatari certification landscape

Qatar (قطر) has built one of the more sophisticated regulatory frameworks in the GCC for product safety and quality. For lighting products entering the country, market surveillance and import requirements are overseen primarily by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry — MoCI (وزارة التجارة والصناعة), with standards work sitting with the national standards body operating under it.

In practice, conformity for lighting products is handled through the GCC-wide G-Mark certification system, while Qatar-specific import requirements are arranged per order through the importer of record under MoCI oversight.

Qatar's certification system has evolved rapidly since the country's infrastructure build-up for the 2022 FIFA World Cup, which drove significant investment in regulatory capacity. Today, the system is mature and enforcement is serious — especially for construction-related products like lighting fixtures specified into commercial and hospitality projects in Doha (الدوحة).

If you are working on a project in Qatar, you need to understand two key certification pathways: the GCC G-Mark (the Gulf-wide conformity mark) and the IECEE CB scheme as its technical foundation.

Conformity assessment in Qatar

Qatar does not operate a standalone national quality mark for lighting products. Conformity for the Qatari market is generally handled through GCC G-Mark certification, with import requirements administered by the Ministry of Commerce and Industry (MoCI) and arranged per order with your local importer.

How conformity works in practice

G-Mark certification for lighting products follows a typical third-party certification model:

  • Application. The manufacturer or their authorized representative submits an application to a GSO-approved notified body.
  • Type testing. Product samples are tested by an accredited laboratory against the applicable GCC technical regulation. For lighting, this is typically based on IEC 60598 (luminaires) and IEC 61347 (control gear).
  • Factory audit. For certain product categories, a factory audit is required to verify that the manufacturer's quality system can consistently produce compliant products.
  • Certification decision. If testing and audit pass, the notified body issues a G-Mark certificate and authorizes use of the G-Mark on the product and packaging.
  • Import arrangements. Qatar MoCI import requirements — documentation, Arabic labeling, and any category-specific conditions — are confirmed per order with the Qatari importer of record.

Is certification mandatory for lighting?

This is the question we get most often. The answer is: it depends on the product category and the project. Categories of lighting products covered by GCC technical regulations require G-Mark certification for market access. Other categories may be required by the project specification or by the client's procurement policy.

Government projects and large-scale private developments in Doha (الدوحة), Lusail (لوسيل), and Al Rayyan (الريان) almost always require G-Mark certified products. Retail products sold directly to consumers in Qatari retail outlets also fall under mandatory certification for many categories.

The safest approach: do not assume a product is exempt. Confirm with the Ministry of Commerce and Industry or with a local certification consultant whether your specific product type requires mandatory certification. And if you are bidding on a project, check the specification documents carefully — the project may require certification even if the general market does not mandate it.

Safety requirements for lighting products

Qatari safety standards for lighting products are largely based on international (IEC) standards, with specific national conditions. The core standards are:

  • QS/IEC 60598-1 — Luminaires — Part 1: General requirements and tests
  • QS/IEC 60598-2-X — Luminaires — Part 2: Particular requirements for specific product types (downlights, track lights, etc.)
  • QS/IEC 61347-1 — Lamp controlgear — Part 1: General and safety requirements
  • QS/IEC 61347-2-13 — Lamp controlgear — Part 2-13: Particular requirements for d.c. or a.c. supplied electronic controlgear for LED modules

Key Qatari national conditions

Qatar's electrical grid operates at 240V / 50Hz. This is different from Saudi Arabia's 230V / 60Hz, and it matters for certification. A product certified for the Saudi market may not automatically qualify for Qatar because of the voltage and frequency difference.

Other national conditions to be aware of:

  • Plug type: Qatar uses Type G (British standard BS 1363) plugs, same as the UK and Saudi Arabia.
  • IP ratings: For outdoor fixtures, Qatar's high temperature and humidity conditions plus occasional dust storms mean that proper IP rating verification is taken seriously.
  • Arabic marking: Product marking and documentation must be in Arabic or bilingual (Arabic + English). This is a market surveillance check item.
  • Thermal performance: With ambient temperatures regularly exceeding 45°C in summer, the operating temperature range of lighting products is a practical concern. Make sure fixtures are rated for the ambient temperatures of the installation location.

Energy efficiency regulations in Qatar

Qatar has been actively expanding its energy efficiency regulatory framework as part of its Qatar National Vision 2030 sustainability goals. The Qatar General Organization for Standardization, working with the Qatar Electricity & Water Company — Kahramaa (كهرباء وماء), has developed energy efficiency standards and labeling requirements for several product categories.

Energy efficiency labeling for lighting

Qatar has implemented an energy efficiency labeling program for lighting products, modeled on the EU energy label system but adapted for Qatari conditions. The program covers LED lamps, fluorescent lamps, and certain categories of luminaires.

The label includes:

  • Energy efficiency class (A to G scale, periodically updated)
  • Rated luminous flux (lumens)
  • Rated power consumption (watts)
  • Rated lifetime (hours)
  • Color temperature indication
  • Arabic and English text

The exact efficiency thresholds for each class are defined in the relevant Qatari standard. They are revised periodically as LED technology improves — the thresholds have been tightened several times since the program launched.

What specifiers need to know

For project-based lighting specifications in Qatar, energy efficiency requirements often come from two sources:

  • Regulatory requirements. Certain product categories must meet minimum efficiency standards and carry the energy label. This is especially enforced for products sold through retail channels.
  • Project-specific requirements. Many large projects in Qatar — particularly those targeting sustainability certifications like GSAS (Global Sustainability Assessment System) or LEED — impose their own minimum efficacy requirements that exceed the regulatory minimum.

If you are working on a project targeting GSAS certification (developed by the Gulf Organisation for Research and Development — GORD), the lighting energy efficiency requirements are typically stricter than the baseline regulatory standard. Plan for higher efficacy fixtures and factor this into your product selection early.

GCC G-Mark recognition in Qatar

The GCC Conformity Mark, known as G-Mark (علامة الخليج), is the Gulf Cooperation Council's regional conformity mark. Products that carry the G-Mark have been assessed as meeting the relevant GCC technical regulations and should be accepted in all GCC member states, including Qatar.

How G-Mark works

G-Mark is administered by the GCC Standardization Organization — GSO (المؤسسة الخليجية للمواصفات). It covers products that fall under GCC technical regulations. For lighting products, the GCC has adopted several technical regulations that align with international best practices.

G-Mark certification follows the same general pattern as national marks: type testing, factory audit (for certain categories), and ongoing surveillance. The key difference is that once you have G-Mark, it is theoretically valid across all six GCC member states.

Qatar's acceptance of G-Mark

Qatar is a member of the GSO and recognizes G-Mark certified products for most categories where a GCC technical regulation exists. In practice, this means:

  • If a lighting product has a valid G-Mark certificate issued by a GSO-approved certification body, it should be accepted for import and sale in Qatar without needing additional national certification.
  • However, you should always verify that the specific product category is covered by a GCC technical regulation and that the G-Mark certificate is from an approved issuing body.
  • Some projects may still impose documentation requirements beyond G-Mark, depending on the consultant or client's procurement policy.

Practical advice: G-Mark coverage

If you are selling to multiple GCC markets, G-Mark certification is generally more cost-effective than pursuing country-by-country approvals. For Qatar, G-Mark certification combined with MoCI import arrangements — confirmed per order with your local importer — is the standard route.

Customs clearance process

Lighting products entering Qatar go through the standard customs clearance process, with additional conformity assessment verification for regulated products. Here is how it works:

Step 1: Pre-arrival documentation

Before the shipment arrives at Hamad Port (ميناء حمد) or Doha International Airport (مطار الدوحة الدولي), ensure you have:

  • Commercial invoice with HS codes, values, and product descriptions
  • Packing list
  • Bill of lading or air waybill
  • Certificate of origin
  • G-Mark certificate (if applicable)
  • Test reports and conformity documentation
  • Importer's commercial registration and Qatar Chamber membership

Step 2: Customs declaration

The importer or their licensed customs broker submits the customs declaration through Qatar Customs' electronic system (Al Nadeeb portal). The declaration includes HS code classification, declared value, and country of origin.

Step 3: Conformity assessment verification

For products under mandatory certification, customs verifies that the shipment has the required G-Mark certification. This may involve checking the certificate against the GSO database.

Step 4: Duty and tax payment

Qatar applies a 5% customs duty on most lighting products (under the GCC Common Customs Tariff). Qatar has not implemented VAT — as of 2026, no VAT is in force, unlike Saudi Arabia's 15%. Your landed cost is therefore the customs value plus duty, with no VAT added.

Step 5: Release and delivery

Once all checks are passed and duties/taxes are paid, the shipment is released for delivery. For project cargo, this typically involves transport to the project site in Doha (الدوحة), Lusail (لوسيل), or wherever the construction is taking place.

Doha project lessons learned

Having supplied lighting for numerous projects in and around Doha (الدوحة), we have accumulated a practical understanding of what works and what does not. These are the key lessons:

1. Project specifications often exceed regulatory minimums

Qatar's high-end hospitality and commercial projects — particularly in West Bay, The Pearl, and Lusail (لوسيل) — frequently have specification requirements that go well beyond what the regulation mandates. This includes higher efficacy, stricter color quality (CRI 90+ is common), specific glare metrics (UGR, ULOR), and detailed photometric performance requirements. Do not assume that regulatory compliance equals specification compliance.

2. Approval processes are thorough

Qatari project consultants and government authorities take the submittal and approval process seriously. You will need to provide test reports, certificates, photometric files, product samples, and sometimes mock-ups. The approval cycle can take 4–8 weeks for a typical lighting package. Build this into your project schedule.

3. Local agent relationships matter

Having a strong local partner or agent in Qatar who understands the regulatory landscape, knows the key people, and can navigate the approval process is invaluable. This is not about cutting corners — it is about knowing how the system works and what each authority expects. A good local partner can prevent delays that come from not knowing the unwritten rules.

4. Summer logistics are real

Qatar's summer temperatures (regularly exceeding 45°C from June through August) affect everything from construction schedules to product handling. Lighting products stored in unconditioned containers or warehouses can see temperatures well above their rated operating range. Ensure that sensitive electronic components (drivers, controllers) are not left sitting in a shipping container in the sun for weeks during peak summer.

Common certification issues

These are the problems we see most frequently with lighting certification for Qatar:

  • Assuming Saudi certification works for Qatar. SASO 2902 certification is for Saudi Arabia. While the underlying IEC standards are similar, Qatar has its own import requirements. Saudi certificates are not directly accepted for G-Mark in Qatar.
  • Voltage/frequency mismatch. Products tested and certified for 230V/60Hz (Saudi) may not have valid certification for 240V/50Hz (Qatar). Check the certificate's rated parameters.
  • No authorized representative. A local authorized representative in Qatar is typically needed for certification applications and import arrangements. If you are an overseas supplier, you need a local partner to act as your representative.
  • Certification in the wrong company name. The certificate must be in the name of the manufacturer or their authorized representative. A certificate issued to a trading company that is no longer involved is not valid.
  • Expired certificates. G-Mark certificates have validity periods (typically 3–5 years) and require renewal. Check the dates.
  • Arabic documentation missing. Installation instructions, product datasheets, and safety information must be available in Arabic for the Qatari market.

Qatar lighting certification checklist

Before you ship lighting products to a project in Qatar, run through this list:

Qatar lighting certification checklist

  • G-Mark certification obtained (verify if mandatory for your product category)
  • Certification covers 240V / 50Hz Qatari grid conditions
  • Test reports from accredited laboratory (IECEE CB or equivalent)
  • Energy efficiency requirements met (regulatory minimum + project requirements)
  • Arabic product marking and documentation provided
  • Local authorized representative in place (if required)
  • Certificate is current and not expired
  • All product variants listed on the certificate
  • Commercial invoice and customs documentation prepared
  • Project-specific specification requirements reviewed and met

Qatar's certification system is structured and professional. It is not as fast as some markets, but it is predictable once you understand the process. The key is to start early — certification always takes longer than you think it will, and rushing it costs money.
