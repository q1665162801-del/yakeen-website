Why go custom?

For most homes, a beautiful off-the-shelf chandelier works perfectly well. But when you're building a high-end villa with double-height ceilings, a grand majlis, and interiors designed by a top studio, a catalog chandelier starts to feel like a compromise.

Custom chandeliers aren't just about being "fancy." They're about creating a piece that belongs to your space — perfectly proportioned, perfectly scaled, perfectly matched to the architecture and interior design. It's the difference between a nice light fixture and a signature piece that defines the entire room.

You should consider a custom chandelier if:

  • You have a double-height or unusually shaped space that standard fixtures don't fit
  • Your interior designer has a specific vision and nothing on the market matches
  • You want the chandelier to be the statement piece of the home
  • You need specific dimensions, materials, or finishes that aren't available ready-made
  • You're working with a specific design theme (Arabic, modern, classical, minimalist) and want everything to cohere

When to start the process

This is the single most common mistake. Clients wait until the interior is almost finished, then realize they need a chandelier and call us. That's too late.

A custom chandelier isn't just a product you bolt to the ceiling — it's a piece that should be integrated into the architecture. The ceiling structure, the electrical point, the access hatch for maintenance, the lift or rigging for installation — all of these need to be planned for in advance.

Ideal timeline: Start the design process during the schematic design or design development phase, when the architecture is still being finalized. That gives us 6-9 months for design, engineering, manufacturing, and delivery — perfectly aligned with a typical villa fit-out timeline.

The custom chandelier design process

Every project is different, but we follow a consistent six-stage process to make sure nothing falls through the cracks:

  1. Brief & concept
  2. Design development
  3. Engineering & prototyping
  4. Final design & approval
  5. Manufacturing & quality control
  6. Delivery & installation

Let's walk through each stage in detail.

Design development

Once the concept is approved, we move into detailed design. This is where the idea becomes a real, buildable object.

What happens in this stage

  • Detailed 3D modeling. We build a full 3D model of the chandelier in SolidWorks, including every component. This lets us check proportions, structural integrity, and how it will look from every angle.
  • Scale drawings. Front, side, and plan views with precise dimensions. These go to the structural engineer and the MEP team for coordination.
  • Material samples. We prepare physical samples of every material and finish — metal finishes, glass types, crystal, fabric, whatever the design calls for.
  • Photometric study. We run DIALux calculations to predict exactly how much light the chandelier will produce, how it will distribute in the room, and what the effect on the space will be.
  • Structural analysis. Especially important for large, heavy pieces. We calculate the weight, the load on the ceiling, and specify the mounting hardware required.

At the end of this stage, you have a complete design package — drawings, renders, material samples, photometrics, and a fixed price. Every element is specified and documented.

Engineering & prototyping

This is where many custom chandelier projects fail. A beautiful design is only useful if it can be built safely, reliably, and to the standard you'd expect in a luxury home.

Key engineering considerations

  • Thermal management. LED drivers and light sources generate heat. In a closed chandelier body, heat builds up and shortens the life of the LEDs. We do thermal simulations to ensure temperatures stay within safe limits.
  • Structural integrity. A chandelier hanging 10 meters above a marble floor can't fail. Every component — from the ceiling mount to the individual wires — is engineered with a safety factor of at least 5x.
  • Electrical safety. All wiring must be properly rated, insulated, and routed. For GCC projects, we design to SASO and IEC standards to ensure certification compliance.
  • Maintainability. Bulbs burn out. Drivers fail. A beautiful chandelier that requires a crane to change a light source is bad design. We build in access panels, modular components, and easy-to-reach drivers.
  • Weight & mounting. Large chandeliers can weigh hundreds of kilograms. The ceiling structure needs to be designed to handle the load, and we provide detailed mounting specifications to the structural engineer.

Prototyping

For complex or unusual designs, we engineer a full-scale prototype (or a detailed mock-up of the critical section) before committing to full production. This catches design issues early — it's much cheaper to modify a prototype than to rework a finished piece.

Materials & finishes

The materials you choose define the character of the chandelier — and the quality of the materials defines how good it will look in 10 years. Cheap finishes tarnish, cheap glass looks cloudy, and cheap metalwork has visible welds.

Common materials for luxury villa chandeliers

  • Brass. The classic choice. Polished, brushed, aged, or patinated — brass ages beautifully and works with almost any interior style. Solid brass is heavy and substantial; brass-plated steel is a cost compromise that never looks as good.
  • Stainless steel. Modern, sleek, and durable. Brushed nickel and polished chrome are both stainless steel finishes. Good for contemporary and minimalist interiors.
  • Glass. Murano glass, hand-blown glass, cast glass, slumped glass — each has its own character. Hand-blown glass has subtle variations that make each piece unique.
  • Crystal. Traditional, elegant, and undeniably luxurious. Asfour and Swarovski are the standards, but there are many quality levels. For a custom piece, we specify exactly what grade of crystal to use.
  • Fabric & silk. Soft, warm, and textural. Great for creating a more intimate, residential feel. We use fire-rated fabrics for safety compliance.
  • Natural materials. Wood, stone, shell, leather — anything is possible with custom design. The key is making sure the material is appropriate for the application (heat, humidity, UV exposure).

Manufacturing & quality control

A great design can be ruined by bad manufacturing. The difference between a custom chandelier that looks like art and one that looks like a DIY project is in the details — the welds, the finish, the alignment, the wiring.

Our quality control process

  1. Incoming material inspection. Every sheet of metal, every piece of glass, every LED module is checked when it arrives. Materials that don't meet spec get sent back.
  2. In-process checks. Quality checks at each stage of production — after cutting, after welding, after finishing, after assembly. Problems caught early are cheap to fix.
  3. Full assembly & test fit. Before shipping, we assemble the entire chandelier at the production base. We check that every piece fits, every connection is solid, and the whole thing is straight and level.
  4. Aging test. A minimum 4-hour burn-in is specified for custom units (extended cycles on request), so early driver failures are caught before packing.
  5. Final inspection & photos. We take detailed photos of the finished piece from multiple angles and send them to the client for approval before it's packed for shipping.
  6. Packing for export. Custom crates with foam lining, clearly labeled, with assembly instructions and spare parts included.

Installation & commissioning

The installation is where it all comes together — and where things can go very wrong if you're not careful. A beautiful chandelier installed crookedly or with visible wires is a disappointment.

What to expect on installation day

  • Site preparation. The area below needs to be clear and protected. For large pieces, we may need scaffolding or a lift.
  • Rigging & lifting. Heavy chandeliers can't just be carried up a ladder. We use proper rigging equipment and work with experienced installers.
  • Mounting & alignment. The chandelier must be perfectly level and centered over the table or room. Even a centimeter off is noticeable.
  • Electrical connection. Done by a qualified electrician, tested for safety, and verified that all circuits work correctly.
  • Focusing & aiming. If the chandelier has adjustable elements (spotlights, uplights, etc.), we aim them on site to get the exact effect we designed for.
  • Scene setting. We set the dimming levels, program the scenes into the control system, and make sure everything looks perfect.

For projects where we're doing the full lighting package, we handle all of this. If you're working with your own installer, we provide detailed installation instructions and can send a supervisor for the critical first day.

How much does a custom chandelier cost?

This is the question everyone asks, and there's no simple answer — custom means custom, and the range is enormous. Every piece is quoted privately against its design, materials, and engineering scope, but it helps to understand the tiers:

  • Entry-level custom: A modified standard design with custom sizing or finishes. Small to medium size.
  • Mid-range custom: Fully custom design, premium materials, medium to large size. Typical for a dining room or secondary majlis.
  • High-end custom: Large statement pieces for double-height spaces, complex designs, luxury materials. Typical for a main majlis or grand entry.
  • Flagship bespoke: Very large, extremely complex, often sculptural. These are art pieces that happen to produce light.

The biggest cost drivers are size (bigger = more material + more labor), complexity of design, materials (solid brass is more expensive than steel, crystal is more expensive than glass), and whether tooling or molds are required.

How long does it take?

Again, it depends on complexity, but here's a typical timeline for a mid-to-high-end custom chandelier:

  • Concept & design: 2-4 weeks. Brief, sketches, concept renders, revisions.
  • Engineering & detailing: 2-3 weeks. 3D modeling, structural calculations, material specs.
  • Prototype (if needed): 2-4 weeks. Mock-up, review, revisions.
  • Manufacturing: 6-10 weeks. Depending on size and complexity.
  • Quality control & finishing: 1-2 weeks. Assembly, testing, inspection, packing.
  • Shipping & delivery: 1-3 weeks. Sea freight is slower but cheaper; air freight is faster but costs more.
  • Installation: 1-5 days. Depending on size and complexity.

Total: 12-20 weeks from start to finish. For simpler pieces, it can be faster. For very complex pieces, allow 6-8 months.

Common pitfalls

We've seen almost everything that can go wrong with a custom chandelier project. Here are the most common mistakes and how to avoid them:

  1. Starting too late. The #1 problem. By the time you call us, the ceiling is done, the electrician is waiting, and there's no time to do it properly. Start early — ideally during design development.
  2. Wrong scale. A chandelier that's too small for the room looks sad and insignificant. A chandelier that's too big overwhelms the space. A good lighting designer will help you get the proportions right.
  3. Forgetting about weight. That beautiful 300kg chandelier won't stay up if the ceiling structure wasn't designed for it. We provide detailed structural requirements — make sure your engineer sees them.
  4. No access for maintenance. LEDs last a long time, but drivers fail. If you can't reach the driver without dismantling half the chandelier, that's a problem. Good design includes maintainability.
  5. Choosing purely on price. Custom chandeliers are not a commodity. A cheaper quote usually means cheaper materials, worse craftsmanship, and no engineering. You get what you pay for.
  6. No dimming control. The brightest version of any chandelier is rarely the best-looking version. Always dim — even chandeliers with candle-style bulbs look better at 70% brightness.
