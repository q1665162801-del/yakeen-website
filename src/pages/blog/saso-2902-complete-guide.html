What is SASO 2902?

SASO 2902 is the Saudi technical regulation for LED lighting products, issued by the Saudi Standards, Metrology and Quality Organization — SASO (الهيئة السعودية للمواصفات والقياس والجودة). It sets the mandatory safety, energy efficiency, and labeling requirements that all LED lighting products must meet before entering the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia (المملكة العربية السعودية).

The regulation was introduced to replace a patchwork of older standards and bring Saudi requirements in line with international best practices while adding specific national conditions. It applies to a wide range of LED lighting products used in residential, commercial, and industrial settings.

SASO 2902 is not optional. It is a legal requirement enforced through customs controls and market surveillance. Products that do not comply can be held at ports, rejected, or destroyed — and the importer is liable for any associated costs.

Why SASO 2902 matters for lighting projects

We have seen projects derailed because someone assumed "CE certification is close enough" or "the supplier said it would be fine." Here is what actually happens when fixtures fail SASO 2902 compliance:

  • Customs detention. Shipments get held at Jeddah Islamic Port or King Abdulaziz Port Dammam until documentation is resolved. Typical delay: 2–6 weeks.
  • Financial penalties. Fines can range from a few thousand riyals to significant percentages of shipment value, depending on the nature and severity of the non-compliance.
  • Product re-export or destruction. In serious cases, SASO orders the products re-exported or destroyed at the importer's expense.
  • Project delays. A single detained container can push back a fit-out schedule by weeks, triggering liquidated damages clauses.
  • Reputational damage. Repeated non-compliance flags can lead to heightened scrutiny on future shipments from the same importer.

The cost of getting it wrong is far higher than the cost of getting it right. Verification takes a few hours of work. A customs detention costs tens of thousands of dollars and weeks of schedule.

What SASO 2902 covers

SASO 2902 addresses three broad categories of requirements: safety, energy efficiency, and marking.

Safety requirements

The safety provisions of SASO 2902 are based on IEC 60598 (luminaires) and IEC 61347 (control gear), with Saudi national differences. Key areas include:

  • Electrical insulation and dielectric strength
  • Protection against electric shock (Class I, II, III requirements)
  • Thermal resistance and fire safety
  • Mechanical strength and impact resistance
  • Ingress protection (IP) rating verification
  • Resistance to dust and moisture for outdoor-rated fixtures

Energy efficiency requirements

Energy efficiency is where many products fall short. SASO 2902 sets minimum efficacy requirements (lm/W) for different product categories and requires registration under the SASO Energy Efficiency Labeling Program. Products are classified into efficiency bands, and only products meeting the minimum threshold are allowed entry.

Key efficiency requirements include:

  • Minimum luminous efficacy per product category
  • Power factor requirements (typically ≥ 0.9 for most commercial fixtures)
  • Standby power limits
  • Rated lifetime and lumen maintenance claims

Marking and labeling requirements

SASO 2902 specifies what must be marked on the product itself and on the packaging. Marking must be durable, legible, and in Arabic or both Arabic and English. Required markings include:

  • Manufacturer name and trademark
  • Model number or type designation
  • Rated voltage, frequency, and power
  • IP rating (where applicable)
  • Energy efficiency label
  • Country of origin
  • Warning symbols as appropriate

Which lighting products need SASO 2902?

SASO 2902 covers a broad range of LED lighting products. If you are specifying any of these for a project in Saudi Arabia, they must comply:

  • LED downlights (مصابيح سفلية) and recessed luminaires
  • LED track lighting (إضاءة مسارية) and spotlights
  • LED panel lights and ceiling fixtures
  • LED linear lighting systems
  • LED wall washers and floodlights
  • LED streetlights and outdoor area lighting
  • LED lamps and bulbs (A-shape, PAR, MR16, etc.)
  • LED drivers and control gear (integrated or separate)
  • LED strips and flexible lighting
  • Emergency lighting fixtures

Some specialty lighting products — such as stage lighting, medical lighting, and automotive lighting — fall under different regulations. If you are unsure whether a product is covered, check with SASO directly or ask your supplier for the specific regulation reference.

The SASO 2902 testing process

SASO 2902 certification operates through the IECEE CB scheme, which means testing done in an IECEE-accredited laboratory is recognized by SASO, subject to verification of Saudi national differences. Here is how the process works:

Step 1: Product testing

The manufacturer submits product samples to an IECEE-accredited testing laboratory. The lab tests according to the relevant IEC standards (e.g., IEC 60598-1 for general luminaire safety, plus the applicable part 2 standard for the specific product type). The lab also tests energy efficiency performance.

Step 2: CB report and certificate

If testing passes, the lab issues a CB Test Report and CB Test Certificate. These documents serve as the foundation for SASO recognition.

Step 3: Application to SASO

The manufacturer or their authorized representative applies to SASO for recognition. The application must include the CB documents, a declaration of conformity, product documentation, and evidence of compliance with Saudi national differences.

Step 4: SASO review and national differences

SASO reviews the application and the CB report. They verify that the product meets any Saudi-specific requirements that go beyond the base IEC standard. This can include voltage (230V/60Hz), plug types, and specific marking requirements in Arabic.

Step 5: SASO IECEE Recognition Certificate

If everything checks out, SASO issues an IECEE Recognition Certificate. This certificate confirms that the product complies with SASO 2902 and is eligible for the SABER platform (منصة سابر) registration and customs clearance.

Step 6: Energy efficiency registration

Separately, the product must be registered under the SASO Energy Efficiency Labeling Program. This involves submitting efficacy test data and receiving an energy efficiency rating.

SASO 2902 vs. old SASO standards

Before SASO 2902, lighting products were covered by a mix of older SASO standards. The transition to SASO 2902 brought several key changes:

  • Consolidation. SASO 2902 consolidates requirements that were previously spread across multiple standards into a single technical regulation.
  • IECEE alignment. The regulation explicitly adopts the IECEE CB scheme as the primary certification pathway, reducing redundant testing.
  • Tighter energy efficiency. Minimum efficacy thresholds have been raised compared to older standards. Products that passed under the old rules may not meet the new bar.
  • Stricter marking. Arabic language requirements on product marking are more clearly defined and more strictly enforced.
  • SABER integration. SASO 2902 compliance is verified through the SABER platform, creating a digital audit trail from certification to customs clearance. Our SABER registration guide covers this in detail.

If you have products certified under older SASO standards, you need to check whether they have been transitioned to SASO 2902. Older certificates are not automatically valid under the new regulation.

Common SASO 2902 pitfalls

These are the most frequent mistakes we see on lighting projects in Saudi Arabia (المملكة العربية السعودية). Some are obvious in hindsight. Others are subtle and have caught experienced teams off guard.

1. Family certification assumption

A supplier shows you a SASO 2902 certificate for one model in a product family and claims the whole family is covered. It is not. SASO 2902 certification is model-specific. Each model number, each wattage variant, and each optical configuration needs to be listed on the certificate or covered by the test report's scope.

2. CB report only, no SASO recognition

"We have a CB report" is not the same as "we have SASO 2902 compliance." A CB report is the input to the SASO process, not the output. The product must go through SASO's review and receive an IECEE Recognition Certificate. CB reports from labs that are not IECEE-accredited are not accepted at all.

3. Energy efficiency overlooked

Safety compliance gets all the attention, but energy efficiency is where many products actually fail. A fixture with solid safety certification but low efficacy will still be rejected. Always verify both safety and energy efficiency registration — they are separate requirements.

4. Arabic marking missing or incorrect

Products arrive with English-only labeling, assuming it will "probably pass." It won't. SASO 2902 requires Arabic on the product marking. The Arabic must be accurate — not just Google Translate output. We have seen shipments held because a warning symbol caption was mistranslated.

5. Driver certified, luminaire not

"The LED driver has SASO certification" does not mean the complete fixture is certified. The luminaire as a whole — housing, optics, wiring, driver assembly — must be tested and certified as a complete product. Component-level certification does not substitute for product-level certification.

6. Wrong voltage or frequency

Saudi Arabia uses 230V / 60Hz. Products designed for 220V/50Hz or 120V/60Hz markets may have test reports based on those electrical conditions. The certification must be for the correct voltage and frequency for the Saudi market. Always check the rated parameters on the certificate match 230V/60Hz.

7. Expired certificates

SASO 2902 certificates have expiration dates. Suppliers sometimes keep using certificates that expired months ago because renewal is in process. Expired certificates are not valid for customs clearance. Check the date.

8. Private label / white label mismatch

If a supplier is selling another company's product under their own brand name, the SASO 2902 certificate must be issued to (or transferred to) the brand that appears on the product. A certificate in Factory A's name does not cover a product sold under Brand B's label.

How to verify SASO 2902 compliance

Do not take a supplier's word for it. Verify compliance yourself. Here is the process we use:

1. Request the actual certificates

Ask for the full documentation package:

  • SASO IECEE Recognition Certificate (PDF)
  • CB Test Report and CB Certificate
  • Energy efficiency registration confirmation
  • Declaration of Conformity

2. Check the certificate details

When you receive the certificates, verify:

  • Model number matches. The exact model you are ordering must be listed.
  • Certificate is current. Check the issue and expiry dates.
  • Issuing authority is SASO. Not a third party, not a "consultant."
  • Scope covers your variant. Wattage, beam angle, color temperature, driver type — all must be within the certified scope.
  • Voltage and frequency. Must be 230V / 60Hz for Saudi use.

3. Cross-check on the SASO database

SASO maintains a public product conformity database. You can look up the certificate number or product model to confirm it is registered and valid. This takes 5–10 minutes and catches most fake or expired certificates.

4. Verify on physical samples

When you receive sample fixtures, check the product itself:

  • Is the SASO / IECEE mark present?
  • Does the model number match the certificate?
  • Is the marking in Arabic (or Arabic + English)?
  • Are rated parameters clearly labeled?

Final checklist

Before you approve a lighting product for a Saudi project, run through this list:

SASO 2902 compliance checklist

  • SASO IECEE Recognition Certificate obtained and current
  • Exact model number listed on certificate
  • CB Test Report from IECEE-accredited lab
  • Energy efficiency registration confirmed
  • Product voltage/frequency: 230V / 60Hz
  • Arabic product marking present and accurate
  • Certificate holder matches the supplier / brand on product
  • All variants (wattage, beam, CCT) within certified scope
  • SABER product registration (PCoC) in place
  • Certificate not expired
