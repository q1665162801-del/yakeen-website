SASO & G-Mark Certification: What Specifiers Need to Know

SASO 2902, G-Mark, SABER, MOIAT — which you need, how to get it, and what causes customs delays at Jeddah and Jebel Ali.

In this article

  • Why certification matters
  • SASO IECEE Recognition Certificate
  • G-Mark (GCC Conformity Marking)
  • Energy efficiency requirements

Why certification matters

Lighting product certification in Saudi Arabia and the GCC is not a formality. It is a mandatory legal requirement with real consequences for non-compliance. Products that arrive without proper certification can be held at customs, rejected on site, or result in project delays and penalties.

Yet certification confusion is one of the most common problems in lighting specification. Many lighting providers claim their products are "certified" when they are not — or when the certification is for a different model, from an expired certificate, or does not cover the full product range.

For specifiers, understanding the certification landscape is essential. This guide explains what SASO and G-Mark certification actually mean, how to verify that products are properly certified, and what to watch out for.

SASO IECEE Recognition Certificate

The Saudi Standards, Metrology and Quality Organization (SASO) is the national standards body of Saudi Arabia. For electrical and electronic products — including LED lighting — SASO operates an IECEE recognition scheme based on the CB scheme.

How it works

  1. A product is tested by an IECEE-accredited laboratory according to the relevant IEC standard
  2. The laboratory issues a CB Test Report and CB Test Certificate
  3. The company whose product is being certified applies to SASO for an IECEE Recognition Certificate
  4. SASO reviews the CB report and adds any Saudi national differences
  5. If approved, SASO issues the IECEE Recognition Certificate

What products need SASO IECEE?

Most electrical lighting products require SASO IECEE certification for entry into Saudi Arabia, including:

  • LED luminaires (all categories)
  • LED drivers and control gear
  • LED lamps and modules
  • Control equipment (dimmers, controllers)

How long is it valid?

SASO IECEE Recognition Certificates are typically valid for a defined period and require periodic renewal. Always check the expiration date on the certificate — expired certificates are not valid for customs clearance.

G-Mark (GCC Conformity Marking)

The G-Mark is the Gulf Cooperation Council (GCC) conformity mark. It indicates that a product meets the relevant GCC technical regulation and is eligible for placement on the market in all GCC member states (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar, Kuwait, Oman, Bahrain).

G-Mark for lighting products

Lighting products fall under the GCC Low Voltage Electrical Equipment and Appliances technical regulation. The G-Mark certification process involves:

  • Product testing according to GCC standards (based on IEC standards)
  • Production facility quality management system audit
  • Ongoing surveillance (periodic production facility audits and market surveillance testing)

G-Mark vs. SASO IECEE

Both certifications are relevant for Saudi Arabia. They serve different purposes:

  • SASO IECEE — Saudi national certification, based on CB scheme, required for customs clearance
  • G-Mark — GCC-wide certification, includes production facility audit, required for market placement

For a project in Saudi Arabia, you should verify that products have both SASO IECEE recognition and G-Mark certification. Some projects may accept only one — check with the client and the local authorities.

Energy efficiency requirements

Beyond safety certification, lighting products must also meet energy efficiency requirements in Saudi Arabia and the GCC.

Energy efficiency requirements

SASO Energy Efficiency Labeling Program — Lighting products must be registered and display an energy efficiency label. The program sets minimum efficacy requirements and categorizes products by efficiency class.

Key point: Always verify that the specific model and variant you are specifying is registered on the SASO energy efficiency platform. Registration is per model, per variant — not per product family.

How to verify certification

Any lighting provider can say their products are certified. Verifying it takes a bit more work. Here is what to check:

1. Ask for the actual certificates

Do not accept "yes, we are certified" as an answer. Request the actual certificate documents. You should receive:

  • SASO IECEE Recognition Certificate (PDF)
  • G-Mark Certificate (PDF)
  • CB Test Report and Certificate
  • Energy efficiency registration confirmation

2. Verify the certificate details

When you receive certificates, check:

  • Product model. Does the certificate cover the exact model being specified? A certificate for "Series X" does not cover all models in the "X family" — it covers only the specific models listed.
  • Certificate holder name. Is the entity named on the certificate the same as the provider you're working with? Custom engineering arrangements are common in architectural lighting — make sure the certificate holder is the actual producing entity.
  • Issue and expiry dates. Is the certificate current? Expired certificates are not valid.
  • Issuing authority. Was it issued by the actual certification body, or by a third party?
  • Scope. What exactly does the certificate cover? All wattages? All beam angles? All colors? The scope must include the specific variants being specified.

3. Cross-check against official databases

SASO and other GCC bodies maintain public databases of certified products. Verify that the product is listed:

  • SASO Product Conformity Database
  • GSO (GCC Standardization Organization) G-Mark database

4. Verify on sample products

When you receive sample fixtures, check:

  • Is the certification mark on the product itself?
  • Does the model number match the certificate?
  • Is the rated voltage/power consistent with the certificate?

Common pitfalls and how to avoid them

These are the most common certification problems we see on projects:

  1. Family certification. The provider shows you a certificate for one model and claims it covers the whole product family. It does not. Each model must be certified individually.
  2. Expired certificates. Certificates expire. Lighting providers sometimes continue using expired certificates because renewing takes time and resources. Always check the date.
  3. Component certification ≠ product certification. "The LED driver is certified" does not mean the complete luminaire is certified. The assembled product must be certified as a whole.
  4. Private label confusion. If a provider is selling another company's product under their own brand name, the certification must be in place for that brand and model.
  5. Optional accessories not covered. A fixture might be certified, but a specific lens, reflector, or accessory might not be. If the certification was done with a standard reflector and you are specifying a narrow beam lens, verify that variant is covered.
  6. Only safety, not energy. Safety certification (SASO IECEE) and energy efficiency registration are separate requirements. Both are necessary.
  7. Regional version differences. Some product companies make different versions of the same item for different markets. The version specified for the GCC should have GCC-specific certification.
