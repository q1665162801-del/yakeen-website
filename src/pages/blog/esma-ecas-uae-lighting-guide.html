MOIAT ECAS Certification for Lighting in the UAE

What is MOIAT and ECAS?

MOIAT (formerly ESMA) is the Ministry of Industry and Advanced Technology — the federal standards body of the United Arab Emirates (الإمارات العربية المتحدة). It is responsible for setting and enforcing standards for products sold or used in the UAE, including electrical and lighting products.

ECAS stands for Emirates Conformity Assessment System. It is the certification framework through which MOIAT manages product conformity for regulated products. Products that fall under ECAS regulation must be registered and must display the ECAS conformity mark before they can be placed on the UAE market.

ECAS covers a wide range of product categories, from food and cosmetics to electrical equipment and construction materials. For lighting products, ECAS sets requirements for safety, electromagnetic compatibility (EMC), and energy efficiency.

Why ECAS matters for UAE projects

ECAS is not a paperwork exercise. It is a legal requirement enforced at the border and through market surveillance. The consequences of non-compliance are real:

  • Customs detention. Shipments arriving at Jebel Ali, Port Rashid, or any UAE port of entry can be held if ECAS registration is missing or invalid.
  • Product recall. If non-compliant products are found on the market, MOIAT can order a recall and fine the responsible party.
  • Project delays. A detained lighting shipment can push back a fit-out schedule, triggering contractual penalties.
  • Market access restrictions. Repeat offenders can face restrictions on future imports into the UAE.

We have seen project teams assume that because a product has CE marking or SASO certification, it will automatically pass in the UAE. That assumption is wrong. Each market has its own certification regime, and the UAE's is separate from both the EU and Saudi systems.

Which lighting products need ECAS registration?

Most electrical lighting products require ECAS registration for import and sale in the UAE. The regulated categories include:

  • LED luminaires — downlights (مصابيح سفلية), track lighting (إضاءة مسارية), panels, linear systems, spotlights
  • LED lamps and bulbs — all types including A-shape, PAR, MR16, GU10
  • LED drivers and control gear
  • Fluorescent lamps and luminaires (still regulated despite phase-out initiatives)
  • Discharge lamps and HID fixtures
  • Emergency and exit lighting
  • Street lighting and outdoor floodlights
  • Lighting controllers and dimmers

If you are specifying lighting for a project in Dubai, Abu Dhabi, Sharjah, or any other emirate, you should verify ECAS registration for every product on your schedule.

ECAS certification requirements

ECAS certification for lighting products covers three main areas: safety, EMC, and energy efficiency. Products must meet all three to receive ECAS registration.

Safety requirements

Safety requirements are based on IEC standards with UAE national differences. For luminaires, the base standard is IEC 60598-1 plus the relevant part 2 standard for the specific product type. Key safety areas include:

  • Protection against electric shock
  • Thermal performance and fire resistance
  • Mechanical strength and durability
  • Ingress protection verification
  • Insulation and dielectric strength

EMC requirements

Electromagnetic Compatibility (EMC) is a core ECAS requirement — and one that catches out suppliers who are more familiar with systems that do not always require standalone EMC testing. Products must comply with both emissions and immunity standards:

  • Radiated and conducted emissions
  • Electrostatic discharge (ESD) immunity
  • Radiated and conducted immunity
  • Voltage dips and interruptions

EMC testing must be done in an ISO/IEC 17025 accredited laboratory. Self-declared EMC compliance is not accepted for ECAS.

Energy efficiency requirements

The UAE has been progressively tightening energy efficiency requirements for lighting products. The MOIAT energy efficiency program sets minimum efficacy thresholds and requires products to be registered with an energy efficiency rating. Key points:

  • Minimum luminous efficacy (lm/W) varies by product category
  • Power factor requirements for most commercial and industrial fixtures
  • Energy labeling requirements for certain product types
  • Some product categories have been banned entirely for efficiency reasons (e.g., certain types of incandescent and halogen lamps)

The ECAS registration process

The ECAS registration process involves testing, documentation review, and platform registration. Here is how it works for lighting products:

Step 1: Product testing

First, the product must be tested by an MOIAT-recognized or ISO/IEC 17025 accredited laboratory. Testing covers safety and EMC according to the applicable standards. Energy efficiency performance is also tested and documented.

Step 2: Prepare documentation

Gather all required documentation. This includes test reports, technical files, declaration of conformity, manufacturer information, and product labeling samples.

Step 3: Choose a conformity assessment body

MOIAT works with approved conformity assessment bodies (CABs). You select a CAB that is approved for the lighting product category. The CAB reviews your test reports and documentation to verify compliance with UAE requirements.

Step 4: Submit for review

Submit the application through the CAB or directly through the MOIAT e-services portal, depending on the product category and registration route. The CAB reviews the documentation and may request additional information or testing if gaps are identified.

Step 5: ECAS certificate issuance

If the review is successful, MOIAT issues an ECAS certificate of conformity. The certificate is linked to the product model and manufacturer, and is valid for a defined period.

Step 6: Registration and marking

Once certified, the product must be registered in the MOIAT database and must display the ECAS conformity mark on the product itself and on its packaging.

ECAS vs. SASO: What's the difference?

If you work on projects across both Saudi Arabia and the UAE, you need to understand both certification systems. They share similarities but are not interchangeable.

ECAS vs. SASO comparison

  • Both are based on IEC standards and use CB scheme reports as a starting point
  • Both require safety testing and energy efficiency registration
  • ECAS requires explicit EMC testing as part of the certification; SASO includes EMC but the requirements and approach differ
  • SASO uses the SABER platform for shipment-level certification (SCoC); ECAS does not have an equivalent per-shipment certificate system
  • SASO requires Arabic on product marking; ECAS accepts English marking but Arabic labeling is recommended
  • ECAS is administered by MOIAT at the federal level and applies across all seven emirates
  • SASO has a stronger customs enforcement mechanism through SABER; ECAS enforcement operates through a combination of border checks and market surveillance

Common ECAS pitfalls

These are the mistakes we see most often when companies first start supplying the UAE market:

1. Assuming CE = ECAS

CE marking from the EU does not equal ECAS compliance. The standards are similar and often based on the same IEC foundations, but the national differences, the testing requirements, and the administrative process are all different. You cannot take a CE-certified product and assume it will pass ECAS without additional work.

2. EMC testing done by the wrong lab

EMC test reports must come from an MOIAT-recognized or ISO/IEC 17025 accredited laboratory. Reports from in-house testing or non-accredited facilities are not accepted. We have seen projects delayed by 6–8 weeks because the supplier submitted EMC data from their own test chamber.

3. Energy efficiency category confusion

MOIAT has specific product categories for energy efficiency purposes. Assigning your product to the wrong category can result in a higher or lower efficiency threshold than actually applies, and your registration may be rejected. Get the category right before you test.

4. Certificate holder not registered in UAE

Depending on the product category and registration route, ECAS may require the certificate holder or importer to have a UAE presence. If you are an international supplier without a local office, you may need to work with a UAE-based importer or authorized representative.

5. Ignoring Dubai-specific requirements

While ECAS is the federal system, Dubai Municipality has additional requirements for certain product categories used in construction projects. For lighting specified for Dubai projects, check whether there are additional Dubai Municipality approvals required beyond the federal ECAS registration.

6. Outdated test reports

MOIAT has specific requirements about how recent test reports must be. Generally, test reports older than 3–5 years may not be accepted. Standards are also updated periodically — a report based on a superseded standard version will need to be updated.

How to verify ECAS compliance

Do not take a supplier's claim of ECAS compliance at face value. Verify it using these steps:

1. Request the ECAS certificate

Ask for the actual ECAS certificate of conformity, not just a declaration or a claim. The certificate should be issued by MOIAT or by an approved conformity assessment body on MOIAT's behalf.

2. Check certificate details

Verify the following on the certificate:

  • Product model. The exact model you are ordering must be listed.
  • Issue and expiry dates. The certificate must be current.
  • Issuing authority. MOIAT or a MOIAT-approved CAB.
  • Scope. Does it cover the specific variant (wattage, CCT, beam angle) you are specifying?

3. Cross-check on MOIAT database

MOIAT maintains a public database of registered products. You can search by product name, manufacturer, or certificate number to confirm the registration is valid. This is the fastest way to catch fake or expired certificates.

4. Check product marking

When you receive samples or deliveries, check that the ECAS mark is on the product itself. The mark should include the registration number. Products without proper marking can be rejected even if a certificate exists.

5. Verify EMC and energy efficiency separately

ECAS covers safety, EMC, and energy efficiency. Make sure all three are in order. A product might have safety certification but be missing EMC or energy efficiency registration.
