Oman's regulatory landscape for lighting

Oman (عمان) has a well-established but pragmatic approach to product certification. Among the GCC markets, Oman's system is often described as the most straightforward — not because the standards are lower, but because the process is clear, the authorities are accessible, and there is a strong emphasis on practical compliance rather than bureaucratic box-ticking.

The regulatory body responsible for standards and conformity assessment in Oman is the Directorate General for Specifications and Measurements — DGSM (مديرية العامة للمواصفات والمترولوجيا), which operates under the Ministry of Commerce, Industry and Investment Promotion — MOCIIP (وزارة التجارة والصناعة وترويج الاستثمار).

For lighting products specifically, Oman's regulatory framework is built on three pillars:

  • Omani standards (OS standards), largely aligned with IEC international standards
  • GCC G-Mark recognition for products covered by GCC technical regulations
  • Oman Quality Mark (OQM) for products seeking national certification

If you are a project manager working on a lighting project in Oman — whether in Muscat (مسقط), Salalah (صلالة), Sohar (صحار), or Duqm (الدقم) — understanding how these three pillars interact will save you time and prevent clearance delays.

DGSM: the specifications and measurements authority

The Directorate General for Specifications and Measurements — DGSM (مديرية العامة للمواصفات والمترولوجيا) is Oman's national standards body. Its responsibilities include:

  • Developing and publishing Omani Standards (OS)
  • Administering conformity assessment programs
  • Managing the Oman Quality Mark (OQM)
  • Overseeing market surveillance and enforcement
  • Coordinating with GSO and other GCC standards bodies
  • Liaising with Oman Customs on conformity verification

DGSM operates the Oman Central Laboratories, which provide testing services for a range of products including electrical and lighting equipment. They also accredit and approve private laboratories for specific test categories.

One practical point about DGSM: they are approachable. If you have a question about whether a product requires certification or which standard applies, you can typically get a clear answer within a reasonable timeframe. This is different from some larger GCC markets where navigating the bureaucracy can be more challenging.

GCC G-Mark recognition in Oman

Oman was one of the earlier adopters of the GCC Conformity Mark (G-Mark) among the six GCC member states. G-Mark (علامة الخليج), administered by the GCC Standardization Organization — GSO (المؤسسة الخليجية للمواصفات), is widely recognized and accepted in Oman for products covered by GCC technical regulations.

How G-Mark works in Oman

For lighting products that fall under a GCC technical regulation, a valid G-Mark certificate from a GSO-approved certification body is generally sufficient for import and sale in Oman. You do not need a separate national certification.

However, there are some important conditions:

  • Regulation coverage. G-Mark is only valid for products that are explicitly covered by a GCC technical regulation. If there is no GCC regulation for your product type, G-Mark does not apply and you need national-level assessment.
  • Notification. In some cases, DGSM requires that G-Mark holders notify them of products being placed on the Omani market. This is typically a straightforward administrative step but should not be skipped.
  • Surveillance. Even with G-Mark, products on the Omani market are subject to market surveillance by DGSM. If a product fails a surveillance test, G-Mark status does not protect you from enforcement action.

Practical tip: verify G-Mark scope before shipping

Do not assume that because you have G-Mark for one lighting product it covers all your lighting products. G-Mark certification is specific to the product category and the GCC technical regulation it falls under. Check the exact scope of your G-Mark certificate and confirm with DGSM if you are unsure whether it covers all the products in your shipment.

Oman Quality Mark (OQM)

The Oman Quality Mark (OQM) is Oman's national product certification mark. It is administered by DGSM and signifies that a product meets the applicable Omani standards and technical regulations.

When you need OQM

OQM certification is typically required when:

  • The product does not fall under any GCC technical regulation (so G-Mark is not available)
  • The project specification explicitly requires Omani national certification
  • You are selling directly to Omani government or public-sector clients who prefer national certification
  • You want a competitive differentiator in the Omani market

OQM certification process

The OQM certification process follows the standard third-party certification model:

  1. Application. Submit an application to DGSM or a DGSM-authorized certification body.
  2. Document review. DGSM reviews product documentation, test reports, and manufacturing information.
  3. Type testing. If existing test reports are insufficient or from unrecognized labs, product samples are tested by an approved laboratory.
  4. Factory audit. For certain product categories, a factory audit is required to verify quality management systems and production controls.
  5. Certification. If all requirements are met, DGSM issues an OQM certificate and authorizes use of the mark.
  6. Surveillance. Periodic factory audits and market surveillance testing to maintain certification.

IECEE CB acceptance

Oman is a member of the IECEE CB scheme. CB test reports and certificates from IECEE-accredited laboratories are generally accepted by DGSM as the basis for OQM certification, subject to verification of Omani national differences. This means that if you already have a CB report for your product, the OQM certification process is primarily a review and national differences assessment rather than a full retest.

Safety standards and test requirements

Omani safety standards for lighting products are based on IEC standards, with Omani national modifications where appropriate. The key standards are:

  • OS/IEC 60598-1 — Luminaires: General requirements and tests
  • OS/IEC 60598-2 series — Particular requirements for specific luminaire types
  • OS/IEC 61347-1 — Lamp controlgear: General and safety requirements
  • OS/IEC 61347-2-13 — Electronic controlgear for LED modules
  • OS/IEC 62560 — Self-ballasted LED lamps: Safety requirements

Omani national conditions

  • Voltage and frequency: 240V / 50Hz. This is the GCC standard and matches most Gulf countries except Saudi Arabia (230V/60Hz).
  • Plug type: Type G (BS 1363) — the three-pin square pin plug standard across most of the GCC.
  • Arabic language: Product marking, installation instructions, and safety information must be available in Arabic (or bilingual Arabic/English).
  • Climate considerations: Oman's climate is hot and arid, with high humidity along the coast. Outdoor fixtures need appropriate IP ratings and corrosion resistance, especially in coastal areas of Muscat (مسقط) and Salalah (صلالة).
  • Desert environment: Dust and sand ingress is a real concern for exterior fixtures. IP65 or higher is recommended for most outdoor applications.

Energy efficiency and labeling

Oman has been progressively implementing energy efficiency regulations as part of its Vision 2040 sustainability strategy. The Authority for Public Services Regulation — APSR (الهيئة العامة لتنظيم قطاع الخدمات), working with DGSM, oversees energy efficiency standards and labeling for electrical products including lighting.

Current state of lighting efficiency regulation

Oman has adopted energy efficiency standards and labeling requirements for several categories of lighting products. The program includes:

  • Minimum energy performance standards (MEPS) for regulated lighting product categories
  • Energy efficiency labeling with a class rating system
  • Registration requirements for manufacturers and importers
  • Market surveillance to verify compliance

The specific efficiency thresholds vary by product type and are periodically revised upward as LED technology improves. For the most current thresholds, check with DGSM or APSR directly, as these have been updated several times in recent years.

Project-level efficiency requirements

Beyond regulatory minimums, many large-scale projects in Oman impose their own energy efficiency requirements. This is particularly true for:

  • Government and public-sector projects
  • Projects targeting green building certification (Estidama Pearl Rating System or similar)
  • Large hospitality and tourism developments
  • Industrial and infrastructure projects

For example, the Oman Convention & Exhibition Centre in Muscat (مسقط) and the new developments in Duqm (الدقم) have all specified lighting efficiency requirements that exceed the regulatory baseline. Always check the project specification for specific efficacy and power density targets.

Customs clearance process

Lighting products entering Oman go through the following general clearance process:

Step 1: Documentation preparation

Before the shipment arrives at Port Sultan Qaboos (ميناء السلطان قابوس) in Muscat or the Port of Salalah (ميناء صلالة), ensure you have:

  • G-Mark or OQM certificate (as applicable to your product category)
  • Test reports from accredited laboratories
  • Commercial invoice with HS codes, descriptions, and values
  • Certificate of origin
  • Packing list
  • Bill of lading or air waybill
  • Importer's commercial registration and Oman Chamber of Commerce membership

Step 2: Customs declaration

The importer or their customs broker submits the declaration through Oman Customs' electronic system (Bayan system). The declaration includes HS code classification, country of origin, and declared value.

Step 3: Conformity verification

For regulated products, customs verifies that the shipment has the required conformity documentation (G-Mark certificate, OQM certificate, or equivalent). This may involve checking the certificate against the DGSM or GSO database.

Step 4: Physical inspection (if selected)

Some shipments are selected for physical inspection. Inspections typically focus on verifying that the products match the documentation, that marking is correct, and that there are no obvious safety issues. In rare cases, samples may be taken for laboratory testing.

Step 5: Duty payment and release

Oman applies a 5% customs duty on most lighting products under the GCC Common Customs Tariff. Oman introduced VAT at a standard rate of 5% in April 2021, under the GCC VAT framework. VAT is calculated on the customs value plus duty. Confirm the current tax status before finalizing your costing.

Muscat market characteristics

Having worked on lighting projects across Oman, here is what you need to know about the market — particularly Muscat (مسقط), where most commercial and hospitality projects are concentrated:

Project mix

Muscat's lighting market is driven by several sectors:

  • Hospitality and tourism. Oman's tourism sector is a major economic driver. Luxury hotels, resorts, and tourist developments — particularly along the Muscat coast and in Salalah — create steady demand for high-quality architectural lighting.
  • Infrastructure. Road lighting, public spaces, airports, and transport infrastructure are ongoing areas of investment. Muscat International Airport expansion and the national road building program are examples.
  • Commercial and mixed-use. Office buildings, retail centers, and mixed-use developments in Muscat's business districts.
  • Industrial. The Sohar (صحار) and Duqm (الدقم) industrial zones drive demand for industrial and hazardous-area lighting.

Specifier expectations

Omani lighting specifiers — typically working through international engineering consultants with local offices — have high expectations. They are familiar with global brands and international standards. Certification and documentation are taken seriously, and the submittal process is thorough.

At the same time, the market is relationship-driven. Having a local partner who understands the project landscape, knows the key consultants and contractors, and can provide on-the-ground support is a significant advantage.

Practical considerations

  • Heat is real. Summer temperatures in Muscat regularly reach 45°C+ with high humidity. Fixtures installed in direct sun can see case temperatures well above 60°C. Make sure your product specifications account for the actual operating ambient, not just the rated ambient on the datasheet.
  • Coastal corrosion. Salt-laden air along the Omani coast accelerates corrosion of metal fixtures. Marine-grade finishes or corrosion-resistant materials are recommended for exterior coastal projects.
  • Logistics. Oman's road infrastructure is good, but project sites outside Muscat (like Duqm or the desert interior) have longer lead times for delivery and service. Plan accordingly.

Common pitfalls for first-time suppliers

These are the mistakes we see first-time suppliers to the Omani market make most often:

1. Assuming G-Mark covers everything

G-Mark is well-recognized in Oman, but it only applies to product categories with an active GCC technical regulation. If your product type does not have a GCC regulation, you need OQM or another form of national assessment. Do not assume all lighting products are covered.

2. No local representative

While it is technically possible for a foreign manufacturer to apply for certification directly, the process is much smoother with a local representative or importer who knows the system and can liaise with DGSM on your behalf. Local representation also helps with market surveillance follow-ups and any post-clearance issues.

3. Arabic documentation as an afterthought

Adding Arabic labeling and documentation is sometimes treated as a last-minute task. But if the Arabic is wrong — especially technical terms related to electrical safety — it can cause delays. Get your Arabic reviewed by someone who understands lighting technology, not just a general translator.

4. Underestimating project approval timelines

Omani project consultants take their time with submittal reviews. It is not unusual for the first lighting submittal review cycle to take 3–4 weeks, and there are often 2–3 review cycles before approval. Build this into your schedule from the beginning — do not promise your client a two-week approval.

5. Overlooking coastal environment requirements

Suppliers used to interior markets sometimes underestimate how harsh Oman's coastal environment is on exterior fixtures. We have seen premium European fixtures develop corrosion issues within 2–3 years of installation on Muscat's coast because they were not specified with appropriate marine-grade finishes. Specify for the actual environment, not the datasheet default.

Oman certification checklist

Before you ship lighting products to Oman, work through this checklist:

Oman lighting certification checklist

  • G-Mark certificate obtained (verify product category is covered by GCC regulation)
  • OR: Oman Quality Mark (OQM) obtained for products not eligible for G-Mark
  • DGSM notification completed if required for G-Mark products
  • IECEE CB test reports available with Omani national differences
  • 240V / 50Hz rating confirmed
  • Energy efficiency requirements verified for product category
  • Arabic product marking and documentation present and accurate
  • Local representative or importer in Oman (if required)
  • HS codes correctly classified for customs declaration
  • Fixtures rated for Omani ambient temperatures and environmental conditions

Oman is one of the more accessible GCC markets for lighting suppliers who do their homework. The regulatory system is clear, the authorities are responsive, and the project pipeline is steady. The key to success is respecting the market's professionalism — bring good products, proper certification, and a reliable local partner, and you will do well.
