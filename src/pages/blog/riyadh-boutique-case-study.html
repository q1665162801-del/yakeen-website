Project overview

Location: Riyadh, Saudi Arabia
Project type: 120-key luxury boutique hotel
Architect: Regional architecture studio
Lighting designer: International lighting design consultancy
Typical Yakeen scope: Specification-grade interior and exterior architectural lighting fixtures
Certification: SASO IECEE, G-Mark, energy efficiency registered

A 120-key boutique hotel in central Riyadh is typically designed to offer a distinctive alternative to the large international hotel chains. The design concept emphasizes intimacy, material quality, and a sense of place — drawing on local architectural traditions while providing contemporary luxury.

The lighting design is a critical element of the concept. The lighting designer specifies very particular light quality, beam distributions, and dimming performance. Finding products that meet both the specification and the Saudi certification requirements is the challenge.

The specification challenge

This kind of project typically has several requirements that make off-the-shelf products unsuitable:

  1. Optical precision. The lighting designer specified specific beam angles, spill light characteristics, and glare levels that generic products could not meet. Multiple beam distributions were needed — from very narrow accent to wide wall wash.
  2. Color quality. CRI 90 minimum, R9 > 50, 3-step MacAdam ellipse color consistency. This is well above what standard commercial products offer.
  3. Dimming performance. Smooth, flicker-free dimming down to 1% with DALI control. Standard DALI drivers typically dim to only 5-10%.
  4. Saudi certification. All fixtures required SASO IECEE and G-Mark certification. Many high-quality international brands are not certified for the Saudi market.
  5. Custom finishes. The architect specified custom matte white and bronze finishes to match the interior palette. Standard black or white finishes would not work.
  6. Timeline. The project was on a fast-track schedule. Standard lead times for custom specification-grade products would have delayed the opening.

On projects like this, the initial fixture schedule often includes several international premium brands. When the project team discovers that none of the specified products carry Saudi certification, they need an alternative that can meet the specification without compromising quality.

The engineering approach for this kind of project

For a project of this profile, Yakeen's engineering team would typically be brought in during the design development phase to assess whether our products can meet the specification and, where they cannot exactly match, whether we can engineer solutions that do.

A typical package would include:

  1. Specification review. Going through the entire fixture schedule line by line, comparing each specified product against our catalog and capabilities.
  2. Photometric matching. Where our standard products do not match the specified beam distributions exactly, our optical engineers design custom lenses and reflectors to match the required distributions.
  3. Color specification. Confirming our 90+ CRI, R9 > 50, 3-step binning LEDs meet the color specification — with sample data provided.
  4. Dimming optimization. Working with our driver provider to optimize the DALI dimming curve for ultra-low level dimming, achieving smooth dimming down to 1% without flicker.
  5. Finish matching. Our finishing department develops custom powder coat colors matched to the architect's samples.
  6. Certification confirmation. Confirming that all proposed products — including custom variants — are covered by our SASO IECEE and G-Mark certifications.

Within about three weeks, the design team would typically receive a complete alternative schedule with photometric data, color samples, finish samples, and certification documentation — giving them confidence that the specification can be met with a certified alternative.

Key spaces and solutions

Guest rooms

The 120 guest rooms require a multi-layer lighting scheme: cove lighting for ambient, bedside reading lights, vanity lighting, wardrobe lighting, and a night light scenario. A typical package would include:

  • Linear LED cove fixtures with 2700K, CRI 90+, 3-step binning
  • Adjustable reading spotlights with 15° narrow beam and glare control
  • IP44-rated linear vanity lights with side-mounted diffusers (for flattering face lighting)
  • LED strip lighting for wardrobe interiors, with motion sensor activation
  • All fixtures DALI dimmable, integrated with the guest room management system

Lobby and reception

The double-height lobby requires careful balancing of ambient, accent, and feature lighting. A typical package would include:

  • Recessed downlights with wide beam distribution and low glare for general ambient
  • Narrow-beam adjustable spotlights for artwork and feature walls
  • Wall grazers for the feature stone wall behind reception
  • Linear slot diffusers for ceiling cove lighting
  • All fixtures in custom matte white to match the ceiling

Restaurant

The all-day dining restaurant needs flexible lighting that can transition from bright breakfast to intimate dinner. A typical package would include:

  • Pendant fixtures with diffused warm 2700K light over tables
  • Wall sconces at low level for ambient warmth
  • Accent spotlights for artwork and feature elements
  • Multiple scene settings via the DALI control system

Exterior and landscape

The building exterior and landscape need to feel welcoming and impressive without being over-lit. A typical package would include:

  • Inground uplights for facade columns and landscaping
  • Wall washers for the main entrance facade
  • Path lights for walkways and landscape features
  • All exterior fixtures IP66 rated for Riyadh's dust and heat

DIALux verification and mockup

Before production, the engineering team would build complete DIALux models for all key spaces. This allows the lighting designer to verify light levels, uniformity, and glare before any fixtures are manufactured.

On a project of this profile we would also support an on-site mockup — a full-size mockup of a guest room and a section of the lobby, installed in a warehouse so the design team can see and evaluate the actual fixtures, finishes, and light quality in person.

A mockup like this typically results in refinements such as:

  • Adjustment of the bedside reading light beam angle from 25° to 15° for more focused light
  • Color temperature adjustment of cove lighting from 3000K to 2700K for warmer ambiance
  • Refinement of the custom finish color after comparison with material samples

Making these changes during the mockup phase avoids weeks of rework and ensures that when production fixtures arrive on site, they are correct the first time.

Results and lessons

A boutique hotel delivered to this level of specification typically opens on schedule and earns strong guest feedback on lighting quality — comfortable, well-lit rooms are the highest compliment a lighting design can receive.

Key lessons from this kind of project:

  1. Certification must be checked early. Discovering at tender stage that specified products lack local certification causes delays and compromises. Verify certification during design, not during procurement.
  2. Mockups are essential. No amount of calculation replaces seeing actual fixtures in actual conditions. The mockup caught issues that calculations could not have predicted.
  3. Engineering capability matters more than catalog. A specification partner with engineering capability can match or exceed the specification through custom optics, finishes, and tuning — without sacrificing quality or certification.
  4. Regional support changes the process. Our engineering team operates from Taiyuan, with regional support across the GCC. This means responsive communication and on-the-ground assistance during installation and commissioning.

This scenario shows that specification-grade architectural lighting — with all the quality, precision, and certification that implies — is available in the Saudi market. You do not have to choose between quality and compliance.
