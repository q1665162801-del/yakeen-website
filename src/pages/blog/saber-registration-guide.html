What is SABER?

SABER (منصة سابر) is the Saudi electronic platform for product conformity assessment. It was launched by the Saudi Standards, Metrology and Quality Organization — SASO (الهيئة السعودية للمواصفات والقياس والجودة) — to digitize and streamline the process of verifying that imported products meet Saudi standards before they enter the Kingdom of Saudi Arabia (المملكة العربية السعودية).

Before SABER, product certification and customs clearance involved paper documents, manual checks, and inconsistent enforcement across ports. SABER replaced that with a single digital platform that links product certification, shipment verification, and customs clearance into one system.

For lighting products, SABER is the gateway to the Saudi market. You cannot clear a shipment of lighting fixtures through Saudi customs without the appropriate SABER registration and a Shipment Conformity Certificate.

PCoC vs SCoC

SABER operates with two types of certificates. Understanding the difference is critical because they serve different purposes and are obtained at different stages.

Product Conformity Certificate (PCoC)

The PCoC is a product-level certificate. It confirms that a specific product model meets the applicable Saudi technical regulations (such as SASO 2902 for LED lighting). The PCoC is issued after SASO reviews the product's test reports and certification documentation.

Key points about PCoC:

  • Issued per product model, not per shipment
  • Valid for a defined period (typically 1 year, subject to renewal)
  • Obtained once, then used for multiple shipments
  • Linked to the product's IECEE recognition or G-Mark certification
  • Must be in place before you can get an SCoC

Shipment Conformity Certificate (SCoC)

The SCoC is a shipment-level certificate. It confirms that a specific shipment contains products that are already covered by valid PCoCs. The SCoC is what customs authorities check when your goods arrive at a Saudi port.

Key points about SCoC:

  • Issued per shipment, not per product
  • Valid for a single shipment only
  • Obtained before each shipment arrives in Saudi Arabia
  • Requires a valid PCoC for each product in the shipment
  • Linked to the commercial invoice and bill of lading

Think of it this way: the PCoC is the product's passport, and the SCoC is the visa for each trip. You need the passport first, then you get a visa for each entry.

Which lighting products need SABER registration?

Virtually all electrical lighting products imported into Saudi Arabia require SABER registration. This includes:

  • LED luminaires of all types — downlights, track lights, panels, linear systems
  • LED lamps and bulbs
  • LED drivers and control gear
  • Fluorescent and other discharge lamps (phasing out but still regulated)
  • Emergency and exit lighting
  • Street lighting and outdoor fixtures
  • Lighting control systems and dimmers

Some products fall under low-risk categories and may follow a simplified self-declaration route, but lighting products are generally classified as medium to high risk and require full certification. If you are unsure, check the SABER product category list or consult a local certification body.

Step-by-step SABER registration process

The SABER registration process involves several steps. We recommend starting this process at least 4–6 weeks before you plan to ship.

Step 1: Create a SABER account

The importer (or their authorized representative in Saudi Arabia) must create an account on the SABER platform. You will need a Saudi commercial registration number and other business documentation. If you are an international supplier without a Saudi presence, you will need to work with the importer of record or a local certification consultant.

Step 2: Add products to the platform

Once your account is active, you add each product model to the platform. You will need to provide:

  • Product name and model number
  • Product description and category
  • Manufacturer details
  • HS code
  • Product photos

Step 3: Select a certification body

SABER works with approved certification bodies. You select one from the list available on the platform. For lighting products, choose a body that has experience with SASO 2902 and the IECEE scheme. Price and turnaround time vary between bodies — we have seen differences of 2–3 weeks between the fastest and slowest options.

Step 4: Submit certification documents

Upload the required documentation for each product. This typically includes:

  • SASO IECEE Recognition Certificate or G-Mark certificate
  • CB Test Report and CB Certificate
  • Energy efficiency registration proof
  • Declaration of Conformity
  • Product labeling samples showing Arabic marking

Step 5: Certification body review

The certification body reviews your submission. They check that the documents are valid, that the product meets the applicable regulation, and that the test reports come from accredited laboratories. They may request additional information or documentation if anything is missing or unclear.

Step 6: PCoC issuance

If the review is successful, the certification body issues the Product Conformity Certificate (PCoC). The certificate appears in your SABER account and is linked to your product listing. You can now use this PCoC to apply for SCoCs for individual shipments.

Step 7: Apply for SCoC for each shipment

Before each shipment, you log into SABER, select the products being shipped, enter the shipment details (quantity, value, shipping line, port of arrival), and upload the commercial invoice and bill of lading. The platform generates a Shipment Conformity Certificate (SCoC), which is electronically sent to Saudi customs.

Documents you'll need

Gather these before you start the registration process. Missing documents are the number one cause of delays.

SABER registration document checklist

  • SASO IECEE Recognition Certificate or G-Mark certificate
  • CB Test Report (full report, not just the certificate)
  • CB Test Certificate
  • Energy efficiency registration / SASO energy label confirmation
  • Declaration of Conformity (manufacturer-issued)
  • Product photos (front, back, label close-up)
  • Product datasheet or technical specification
  • Manufacturer business license / registration
  • Importer commercial registration (Saudi)
  • HS code for each product

For SCoC applications per shipment, you will additionally need:

  • Commercial invoice
  • Bill of lading or airway bill
  • Packing list

How long does SABER registration take?

Timelines vary depending on the product category, the certification body, and the quality of your submission. Here is what we typically see for lighting products:

  • PCoC for a new product: 3–6 weeks from complete document submission. Allow 2–3 weeks for document review, plus additional time if the certification body requests corrections or supplementary information.
  • SCoC for a shipment: 1–5 business days, assuming all products have valid PCoCs and the documentation is in order.
  • PCoC renewal: 2–4 weeks before expiry. Start the renewal process early — do not wait until the certificate expires.

These are real-world timelines based on our experience with projects in Riyadh (الرياض) and Jeddah (جدة). The SABER platform itself is generally fast — most delays come from the human review step at the certification body or from incomplete submissions.

Common SABER problems and delays

We have seen shipments delayed for weeks due to avoidable SABER issues. Here are the most frequent ones:

1. Starting too late

The most common mistake. Teams wait until the product is ready to ship before starting SABER registration. If any issue comes up during review — and something usually does — the shipment sits at the port while the problem gets resolved. Start PCoC registration as soon as the product specification is finalized.

2. Incomplete documentation

Submitting a CB certificate without the test report. Or the test report is in Chinese. Or the Arabic label photo is blurry. Every missing or deficient document adds days to the process as the certification body sends it back for correction. Review your documents against the checklist before you upload.

3. Model number mismatch

The model number on the CB report does not exactly match the model number you are registering. Sometimes it is a suffix difference (e.g., "DL-100" vs "DL-100-WW"). Sometimes it is a completely different numbering system. The certification body will flag this and you will need to provide a letter of equivalence from the manufacturer.

4. Wrong product category

Selecting the wrong product category on the SABER platform can route your application to the wrong certification body or trigger requirements that do not apply to your product. Take the time to find the correct HS code and SABER product category. If you are unsure, ask the certification body before you submit.

5. Energy efficiency not registered

SASO IECEE safety certification is in place, but the energy efficiency registration is missing or expired. The certification body will not issue a PCoC without both. Energy efficiency registration is a separate process — don't assume it is included with the safety certification.

6. Certificate holder mismatch

The SABER account is registered to Company A (the importer), but the SASO IECEE certificate is in the name of Company B (the overseas manufacturer). This is normal, but the relationship must be documented. You may need a letter of authorization or a distribution agreement showing the connection.

SABER vs. SASO IECEE

SABER and SASO IECEE are related but not the same thing. Here is how they fit together:

  • SASO IECEE is the product certification itself — the testing and recognition that confirms a product meets Saudi technical regulations like SASO 2902. Read our complete SASO 2902 guide for the full specification details.
  • SABER is the platform through which product certification is verified and linked to individual shipments for customs clearance.

You need both. The SASO IECEE certificate (or G-Mark certificate) is the proof of compliance. SABER is the system that manages the certificates and issues the shipment-level clearance documents that customs actually checks.

Tips for smooth SABER clearance

Based on our experience with dozens of Saudi projects, here is what actually works:

  1. Start early. Begin PCoC registration when you finalize the product selection, not when the product is ready to ship. Build 4–6 weeks of buffer into your project schedule.
  2. Use one certification body if possible. If you have multiple product lines, use the same certification body for all of them. It reduces administrative overhead and they become familiar with your products.
  3. Keep digital files organized. Maintain a folder structure with all certificates, test reports, and SABER confirmations. You will need to reference these repeatedly for each shipment and for renewals.
  4. Check PCoC expiry dates. Set calendar reminders 2 months before certificates expire. Renewal takes time and you do not want a gap.
  5. Verify SCoC is issued before shipment arrives. Do not assume it is automatic. Log into SABER and confirm the SCoC status before the ship docks. If there is a problem, you want to catch it while the ship is still at sea.
  6. Work with suppliers who know SABER. If your lighting supplier has experience with the Saudi market, they will already have PCoCs for their products and can provide the documentation you need without delay. If they do not, you will be teaching them the process at your project's expense.
